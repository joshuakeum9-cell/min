Local-first meeting notes · Windows

Your meetings never leave your machine.

MIN records both sides of a call without a bot joining, transcribes it on your own computer as the meeting happens, and turns the notes you typed into a clean write-up. No account. No API key. No subscription to this. After the one-time model download, recording and transcription never touch the network, and your notes are never uploaded.

How it works

Not a summariser. A way to finish the notes you already started.

A summary written by something that was not in the room reads generic. MIN starts from your notes and uses the transcript to fill them out.

That claim is not yet proven. The test is a blind reader trying to tell a write-up built from your real notes apart from one built from a different meeting's notes. It has never been run.

01 · Record

Both sides, no bot

Your microphone and the call audio are captured as two separate tracks. Nobody sees a bot join the meeting, because none does. Click a meeting on your home screen and the note opens with its title, time and attendees already in it.

02 · Type

Fragments are fine

Half sentences, a heading, a name with a question mark. Your headings become the sections of the write-up, so the structure stays yours.

03 · Write up

One click

Transcription runs on your machine. There is no local model for the write-up: the prompt goes to the assistant you already pay for, by clipboard or into Claude Desktop through the MCP extension.

Why it is built this way

Decisions you can check.

Two tracks, never mixed

"Who said this" is just "which file was it in". A tool that works from one mixed recording has to infer the speaker; MIN keeps two recordings, so there is nothing to infer. No speaker-identification model, no clustering, no guessing, and the two tracks can never drift apart.

Speech recognition stays home

Transcription runs on your CPU, costs nothing, and works with the network cable pulled. It has been benchmarked on exactly one desktop, faster than most laptops, so the specs below are a ceiling rather than a promise.

Your files outlive the app

Every meeting is a folder of plain markdown you can open in Notepad. No database, no proprietary format, nothing to export.

Throwing a note away is undoable

Three dots on any note, then Move to trash. It goes to the recycle bin your computer already has, so it is yours to put back. There is no "are you sure" step, on purpose: the recycle bin is the confirmation, and a dialog in front of something this reversible only teaches you to click through the ones that matter.

The recording is deleted

Audio is removed once transcription succeeds, not merely kept locally. If a transcription fails the audio is kept, because then it is the only way to try again.

The conversation, while it happens

The transcript fills in beside your notes as people speak: the call on the left in grey, your microphone on the right in green, already sorted by who said it. A line lands when its speaker pauses, so a short remark appears a second or two later and a long unbroken stretch takes longer. Those lines are the transcript; nothing is run over the audio a second time.

Your calendar, without a sign-in

Paste the secret iCal address from your calendar settings and your next meetings appear on the home screen. No Google sign-in, no OAuth, no consent screen: it is one HTTPS address MIN reads on a timer, and it works with Outlook, iCloud and Fastmail too. That address is a credential, so it stays on your machine and is never printed in a log.

A nudge, a minute before

Just before a meeting on your calendar, a small card appears in the corner naming it, with a Take notes button that opens a note for that meeting and starts recording, and an X to send it away. Either answer sticks, and it will not raise that meeting again. It cannot take focus, so it never steals the call it is announcing. All-day entries never nudge you, a meeting that started five minutes ago still does, and it does not skip a meeting for having too few people in it, so a lecture or a one-to-one counts like anything else.

You always know it is recording

A round waveform button in the note is grey and still when nothing is captured and green and moving when it is. Switch to the call and a small pill floats above every window with the levels, the clock and a stop button. Drag it anywhere and it stays there. It cannot take focus, so it never interrupts the meeting.

Stop is not the end of the meeting

Stop ends the capture, not the note. Press it again and the recording carries on into the same note as another part of it, whether that is thirty seconds or a day later. One transcript, one clock running through it, and two honest numbers: the audio that was captured, and the wall-clock window it happened in.

A transcript you can work with

Search inside it and matching lines stay with the match highlighted. Click any line to copy it in the file's own format. And a reminder to get consent when transcribing others, where the recording is visible rather than buried in a settings page.

Everything is one search away

The home screen is what is coming up and every note you have taken, newest first. One box searches titles, the notes you typed and the full transcripts, on this machine, over the same markdown files you own.

Specifications

Measured, not estimated. On one machine.

Every number below was measured on one machine, the developer's desktop: an AMD Ryzen 5 5600G, six physical cores, 15.3 GB of RAM, CPU backend. The project's own hardware check refuses to call it a valid test bed. It puts the box at roughly 1.5x faster than the median 4-core laptop, which has never been benchmarked, and warns about that in every result file, three of the four also carrying "validTestBed": false. Read every figure as an upper bound. Raw JSON in results/.

Download

Install it the ordinary way.

  1. Download MIN-Setup.exe and double-click it.
  2. Windows will show a blue “Windows protected your PC” screen. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer. It adds a Start menu entry and a desktop shortcut.
  4. On first launch it downloads 642 MiB of speech models, once. Only after that do recording and transcription work offline.

About that warning

The installer is unsigned: a signing certificate costs money every year and this is a free personal project. Unsigned means Windows cannot confirm who published it, not that anything is wrong with it. Some antivirus engines flag unsigned Electron installers too, usually as a generic heuristic rather than a named threat.

Check what you downloaded: 118,015,127 bytes, SHA-256 6ce1487ea720d38bbed7e8d25b20d60168de4f09cb7f3f534cb5f91d20010858. That hash is published here and on the same release page as the file it describes, so it catches a corrupted or truncated download but not a substituted one, and it is no substitute for a code signature. Windows Explorer shows 113 MB because it counts in binary units. Or build it yourself with npm run dist: a file you built has no mark of the web, so SmartScreen stays quiet, though the result is unsigned too and an antivirus heuristic can still reach it.

Free and open source under the MIT licence. After the first run it makes no network request you did not ask for: a calendar address you pasted, or a write-up you chose to send. No analytics, no account to create.
